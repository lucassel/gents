# Corrections log

Every entry is a real correction from a native speaker of Gents, with the wrong form that
prompted it. The log is the raw material; `SKILL.md` is the distilled rule. When a correction
arrives, add it here **and** fold it into the rule — an entry that never reaches SKILL.md is a
lesson that changes nothing.

Format: what was written → what it should be → why.

| *"meutje" unparsed* | **sarcastic *maatje*, aimed at nobody** | For when *ge slecht komt* or *uw tik aant krijgen zijt*. Announces that things are going badly for you rather than blaming the listener: *meutjeee pff den build is weer gefaald.* |

| — | **is da ier nog serieus ofwa** | Venting after a long run of failures — the twentieth attempt, not the first. "Moet zo wa u eigen uitschijten." Earned by duration; at the first error it reads as theatre. |
| — | **ofwa** | Tag turning a statement into an exasperated question. |
| *"e" recorded as a native softener* | **e = French *hein*** | Both sentence-final tags are French: *e* = hein, *wa* = quoi. The tag system is imported whole, which is deeper than the loanword layer. |
| *sentence-final "wa" read as Dutch "wat"* | **wa = French *quoi*** | "Ja da is letterlijk frans, wa van den quoi." Closes the matter rather than seeking agreement, which is what separates it from *e*. Three homographs: *wat* the question word, *wat* the softener, *quoi* the tag. |
| *"swa raar" unparsed* | **swa = [et] is wa** | The copula swallowed into the softener. Same *wa ne …* as in *zijt wa ne blo* — "a bit of a", not the question word. |
| *"jij is" read as second person with odd agreement* | **it is a real third person** | Address is always *ge/gij*, no exceptions — *jij* is not an address form. *Jij is echt goe bezig* refers to the person standing there as if they were absent, and that is the cut. First logged as "wrong agreement", which missed the point. |
| — | **goe bezig** | Ironic praise, a put-down: "das echt voor te snijden." Dangerous because it is the phrase a learner reaches for meaning it sincerely. |
| *sarcastic caps used in output* | **never produce them** | "Gij moe nooit MERCIIIII doen, das schijte ambetant." Understanding them is the point; writing them is not. (*schijte* = intensifier, shit-.) |
| *the thanks table read as fixed per form* | **delivery beats the form** | Caps and stretched vowels flip any of them to sarcasm, *merci* included: *dankuwel meneer* sincere, *DANKU GAST!!!* and *DANKU POLITIE GENT MERCIIIIIIII* not. Naming the target does it too. |
| *"dank u" logged as merely stiffer* | **dank u often sarcastic, dankuwel sincere** | Three forms: *merci* sincere and default, *dank u* often ironic (*danku three.js*), *dankuwel* sincere again. Length carries it, not language — the first gloss said "French sincere, Dutch ironic", which was a neat line and wrong. |
| — | **fretten / boefen / stekken** | Three verbs for eating, all coarse and all warm: *gaan we eentje fretten in den quick?* |
| `burgertje` | **burgerke** | Diminutive is *-ke* where standard Dutch has *-tje*. Stays *-je* after a t: *frietje*. |
| — | **vurten** | Filthy, morally: *ne rat*. Attaches to a name — *vurten freddy*. |
| — | **ne freddy / ne ziepmuile** | A soap-mouth: "zo ne flauwen plezanten, alstof da jij ziep eeft zitten fretten." Limp, slippery humour. Explicitly *geen koosnaampje*. |
| *an invented Middle-Dutch etymology for "blo"* | **BLO = Bijzonder Lager Onderwijs** | Wrong, and recorded as a warning: a scholarly-sounding derivation conjured from two hearings. The word it was traced to is not Gents at all. *blo* is an acronym for special-needs schooling, used as an insult. "Tis nie schoon ma tis gents." |
| *"da j'en" read as a glide breaking hiatus* | **je-m'en-foutisme** | Wrong inference, recorded because it is instructive: it is French, *je m'en fous* nominalised, not phonology. A hiatus-glide rule had been built on this plus one probable typo — both data points gone, hypothesis dropped. |
| — | **allez komaan** | The strongest of the reaction particles: "da is echt voor alst echt kut is." Not for mild annoyance. |
| — | **kendet** | Sentence tag, *you know?* — not a question. |
| — | **pfff / pfffffff** | Exasperation, in the *ahjo/wajo* family. The number of f's is the force. Do not substitute *zucht* or *ugh*. |
| `ahjo` three replies running | **vary the opener** | *ahjo* and *wajo* mean something specific; as a default opener they wear out. Sometimes *maatje, …*, sometimes nothing. A newly-learned form gets overused immediately — watch for it. |
| `gien haar` | **geenen aar** | *gien* inflects like the other determiners — *gienen/geenen* in the same environment that gives *den* and *nen*. So it is a determiner rule, not an article rule. |
| `een klankregel` | **ne klankregel** | *ne* before a consonant, *nen* before a vowel — the same liaison that gives *de/den*. The old example *nen dak* was wrong twice over: *dak* is neuter, and *d* is a consonant. |
| `de deur` | **den deur** | *Deur* is feminine in standard Dutch and still takes **den** — "DEN DEUR IS DAAR!". The article cannot be predicted from Dutch gender; the *den* list has to be learned. |
| `et is mij beu` | **tis mij beu** | As the subject of a verb, *et* is always *t* — *tis, twerkt, tga, tkan*. It only stays whole in front of a noun: *et water*. |
| `dan kunde et geslacht` | **dan kunde t geslacht / kundet geslacht** | *et* loses its vowel after a vowel and leans back onto the verb. Same anti-hiatus instinct as *den*, opposite move: one adds a consonant, the other drops a vowel. "Ier ist weer half franse regel, door de botsing van de klinkers." |
| `as de eigenlijke regel` | **as den eigenlijke regel** | **den** before a vowel, always, gender aside — *eigenschap* is not even masculine. "Wij doen ier iets lijk et frans, wij doen den als et mee een klinker is." Liaison, same motive as French. |
| `terwijl kik tis al juist heb` | **terwijl dak tis al juist eb** | Every subordinating conjunction takes *da* + fused pronoun, not a standing pronoun: *terwijl dak*, *omdat dak*, *als dak*. |
| `kik` used as the everyday *ik* | **kzou / kga / ik** | *kik* is emphatic and rare — you against somebody else. The default first person is the fused *k-*, or plain *ik*. Overusing *kik* reads as a stutter. |
| `weet je wat` | **wete wa** | Second person fuses as -de/-te: *kunde*, *snapte*, *wete*, *hebde*, *wiste*. |
| — | **diene ledger** | Heard in place of *den ledger*. Wrong by the dialect's own logic and said anyway: "da is gewoon da j'en foutisme van nen gentenaar." Recorded as a licensed slip, not a rule. |
| `twee dingen die kik erbij aangetekend heb, omda de ledger anders liegt` | **twee dingen dak erbij gezet eb, omdat den ledger anders liegt** | Four at once: *die kik* → **dak** (the pronoun fuses onto the complementizer); *aangetekend* → **gezet** (plain verb, not formal); *heb* → **eb** (h-drop, a rule already in the ledger and still dropped in practice); *omda* → **omdat** (conjunctions keep their -t); *de* → **den** (masculine definite article). |

## Activations that arrive as complaints

Recorded because they are easy to misread as ordinary conversation rather than as a request:
*wablief*, *wa gij spreekt is eerder frans*. Neither asks for a language; both say the current
one is not landing.
